A definition of a System

A human cell – a system

  • 29,740 RNA genes; 148,792 gene transcripts; 21,257 protein-coding genes
  • Prediction of the features' behaviour is not reliable enough
  • All the mechanisms are not fully understood
  • Epigenetics
  • Can handle problems that computer scientists are struggling with today

Anderson: “Psychology is not applied biology, nor is biology applied chemistry.” Knowing the laws of the parts does not let you reconstruct the behaviour of the whole.

Game of Life

Born with 3 neighbours
Survives with 2 or 3

Conway's Game of Life, in Gardner, “Mathematical Games”, Scientific American 223 (1970)

A grid of cells, each alive or dead. Every generation, a dead cell with exactly three live neighbours comes alive, and a live cell with two or three live neighbours survives; all others die. From a random start, still lifes, blinking oscillators and gliders that travel across the grid appear. None of these is in the rules. The glider gun emits a new glider every 30 generations. The Game of Life is even Turing complete: a universal computer can be built from it. Click or drag on the grid to add cells.

Flocking

Reynolds, “Flocks, herds and schools”, SIGGRAPH 1987

Each bird only sees its neighbours and follows three rules: separation (don't crowd), alignment (fly the way your neighbours fly), cohesion (move towards your neighbours). No bird knows the shape of the flock. Switch rules off one at a time: without alignment there is no common direction; without cohesion the flock dissolves; without separation it collapses into clumps.

Emergism: Spontaneous oscillations in a communicating cell population

[Wang & Tang, Nat Commun 2019]

Wang & Tang, “Emergence of collective oscillations in adaptive cells”, Nat Commun 10 (2019), doi:10.1038/s41467-019-13573-9. Cells that respond to changes in a shared signal can drive collective oscillations.

The repressilator

Elowitz & Leibler, Nature 403:335 (2000)

Top: one gene repressing itself settles to a steady level. Bottom: three repressors in a ring (lacI ⊣ tetR ⊣ cI ⊣ lacI) oscillate, although no single gene oscillates. Built in E. coli with a GFP readout; the period (hours) is longer than the cell cycle. Simulated with the Elowitz & Leibler model (α = 216, α₀ = 0.216, β = 5, n = 2), same parameters in both panels.

Walkthrough of Course PM

Aim of the course

After passing the course, you should be able to:

  1. Describe widely used statistical and computational techniques employed to investigate data from high-throughput techniques for measuring biopolymers.
  2. Explain the theory of state-of-the-art tools/algorithms for combining data from high-throughput molecular biology experiments.
  3. Choose appropriate machine learning strategies for processing molecular biology data.
  4. Apply network biology tools for interpreting data.
  5. Interpret the results of these analyses in a biologically or medically relevant context.
  6. Reflect over the choice of methods and tools and how it influences the outcome of the analyses.

The modules of the course

The course is organized in 4 modules:

  1. Machine Learning and Statistics – LK
  2. Network Biology and Metabolic Engineering – LK
  3. Multicellular transcriptional systems – SK
  4. Systems genetics of human disease – TL

There is also an introductory lecture. In total 18 lectures and 4 computer exercises.

Prerequisites

The following courses, or equivalent, are recommended:

  • Bioinformatics corresponding to BB2441 Bioinformatics
  • Programming corresponding to BB1000 Programming in Python
  • Probability theory corresponding to SF1911 Statistics for Bioengineering, 6.0 credits
  • Knowledge of modern omics experiments corresponding to BB2255 Applied Gene Technology

We provide some reading recommendations on Canvas, if you feel you lag behind in programming or statistics.

Use of Generative AI

All use of generative AI is prohibited in the course's examination and graded assignments, as it may negatively affect your ability to meet the course's learning objectives.

  • All examinations, computer exercises and graded assignments must be carried out without the help of generative AI.
  • All submissions must be completely human-generated.
  • This includes all debugging of code.

You are fully responsible for everything you submit and must be able to defend and explain it without the support of generative AI. Unauthorized use may result in disciplinary action.

Run the labs in Jupyter Notebook – editors such as VS Code and Colab have AI assistants built in.
